5.6.3. Catapult Case Study

5.6.3.1.

Background and Data
Background Sonoluminescence is the process of turning sound entery into light. An ultrasonic horn is used to resonate a bubble of air in a medium. The medium is usually water. The bubble is compressed by the ultrasound to a hot plasma. A byproduct of this plasmic transformation is the resulting visible light.

In the general physics community, sonoluminescence studies are being carried out to characterize it, to understand it, and to uncover its practical uses. A heated and unanswered question in the comminity is whether sonoluminescence may be used for cold fusion.

NIST's motive for sonoluminescent investigations is to assess its suitability for the dissolution of physical samples. It is believed that maximal dissolution coincides with maximal energy and maximal light intensity. The ultimate motivation for striving for maximal dissolution is that this allows improved determination of alpha and beta emitting radionuclides in such samples.

Eva Wilcox and Ken Inn of the NIST Ionizing Radiation Divison of the Physics Laboratory conducted a sonoluminescent experiment. The purpose of the NIST experiment analyzed in this case study was to determine the important factors which affect sonoluminescent light intensity, and to ascertain optimal settings of such factors which will predictably achieve high intensities. An original list of 49 factors was reduced, based on physics reasons, to a list of seven factors.

Time limitations restricted the experiment to about one month which in turn translated into an upper limit of roughly 20 runs. A 7-factor, 2-level fractional factorial design (resolution 3) was constructed and run

Statistical Goals The case study demonstrates the analysis of a 2(7-3) fractional factorial experimental design. The specific statistical goals of the experiment are:
  1. Determine the important factors that affect sonoluminescence light intensity.
  2. Determine the settings that maximize the sonoluminescence light intensity.
  3. Determine a predicition equation that functionally relates sensitivity to various factors.
Data Used in the Analysis There were seven factors under consideration:
  1. X1 = Molarity (Amount of Solute)
  2. X2 = Solute Type
  3. X3 = ph
  4. X4 = Gas Type in Water
  5. X5 = Water Depth
  6. X6 = Horn Depth
  7. X7 = Flask Clamping
Since the maximum number of runs that could be afforded timewise and costwise in this experiment was n = 20, a 2(7-3) fractional factoral experiment (involving n = 16 runs) was chosen. With an eye to the usual monotonicity assumption in 2-level factorial designs, the selected settings for the seven factors were as follows:
  1. X1 = Molarity: -1 = 0.10 mol, +1 = 0.33 mol
  2. X2 = Solute Type: -1 = Sugar, +1 = Glycerol
  3. X3 = ph: -1 = 3, +1 = 11
  4. X4 = Gas Type: -1 = Helium, +1 = Air
  5. X5 = Water Depth: -1 = Half, +1 = Full
  6. X6 = Horn Depth: -1 = 5 mm, +1 = 10 mm
  7. X7 = Flask Clamping: -1 = Unclamped, +1 = Clamped
The experiment was run with the 16 settings executed in random order. The following data, listed in Yates order, resulted:
