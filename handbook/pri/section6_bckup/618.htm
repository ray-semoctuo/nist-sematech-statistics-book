Dex Contour Plot
DEX Contour Plot In order to determine the best settings for the factors, we can use a dex contour plot. The dex contour plot shows the value of the response variable at the vertices (i.e, the -1 and +1 settings for the factor variables) and indicates the direction that maximizes or minimizes the response variable. The dex contour plot is shown for exactly 2 factor variables. If you have more than 2 significant factors, you may need to generate a series of dex contour plots (each using 2 of the important factors).

In the dex contour plot, we print the value of the response variable (eddy current) at the vertices (i.e., the -1 and +1 settings for the 2 factors). The maximum (or minimum value in some cases) value corresponds to the best settings for the factors. This dex contour plot identifies the best settings as number of turns (X1) equal to -1 and winding distance (X2) equal to 1.

Conclusions The original goals for this study were the following.
  1. Determine the most important factors.
  2. Determine the best settings for the factors.
  3. Determine a good predicition equation for the data.
The full factorial design allowed us to determine that the number of turns (X1) and winding distance (X2) were the most important factors. The Yates analysis allowed us to develop a good prediction equation for the data. In general, full and fractional factorial designs are quite good at answering the first and third questions.

The second question, determining the best settings for the factors, is typically answered by using a response surface design.

Next Step The next step would then be to create a response surface design in the neighborhood of X1=-1 and X2=1 in an attempt to determine optimal values for these factors.
