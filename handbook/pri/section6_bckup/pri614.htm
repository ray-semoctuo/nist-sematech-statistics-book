Main and Interaction Effects: Block Plots
Block Plots Block plots are a useful adjunct to the dex mean plot and the dex interaction effects plot to confirm the importance of factors, to establish the robustness of main effect conclusions, and to determine the existence of interactions. Specifically,
  1. The first plot below answers the question: Is factor 1 important (robust over the 4 combinations of settings of X2 and X3)?

  2. The second plot answers the question: Is factor 2 important (robust over the 4 combinations of settings of X1 and X3)?

  3. The third plot answers the question: Is factor 3 important (robust over the 4 combinations of settings of X1 and X2)?
For block plots, it is the height of the bars which is important, not the relative positioning of each bar. Hence we focus on the size and internals of the blocks, not where the blocks are one relative to another.

In a block plot, each of the 4 bar heights within a plot represent local effect estimates of the factor being focused on. Thus for the first plot below (which focuses on factor 1), the 4 bars all have level 2 of factor 1 higher than level 1 (and thus all 4 effect estimates will be positive), and further, the 4 bars have approximate heights of 2.5, 3, 3, and 3.5, respectively (depending on the X2 and X3 combination). These are 4 local estimates of the factor 1 effect, and these 4 local estimates coalesce into a single global least squares estimate for factor 1 (already given) of +3.1025. Thus from this first plot we see that the least squares estimate for the factor 1 effect is "robust" over the other 4 settings, that is, it does not appear to change (that is, interact) with the other 2 factors, and hence we conclude that the X1*X2 and X1*X3 interaction effects will also be relatively small.

Plot 2 below focuses on the factor 2 effect. Note how all 4 bars have level 1 above level 2 (hence the factor 2 effect will be negative), and note how the 4 bar heights are approximately 1, .75, 1, and .5. These coalesce into the global least squares estimate for factor 2 (given previously) of -0.8675. Note further that when X1 = -1, the bar heights decrease from 1 to .75, and when X1 = +1, the bar heights also decrease (from 1 to .5) Thus regardless of the X1 setting, we see that the X2 effect (the bar height) changes depending on whether X3 is -1 or +1, this is a manifestation of a X2*X3 interaction, and the 2 approximated decreases (-.25 and -.50) coalesce into the exact least squares estimate for the X2*X3 effect of -0.2975.

In summary, the value of the block plot is that is provides additional supporting evidence of the relative importance of the main effects, and also gives a concrete illustration of the existence and magnitude of various interactions.

Conclusions from the Block Plots We make the following conclusions from the block plots:
  1. Relative Importance of Factors: All of the bar heights in plot 1 are bigger than the bar heights in plots 2 and 3. Hence factor 1 is more important than factors 2 and 3.

  2. Important Factors: For plot 1, level 2 is higher than level 1 for all four bars. Likewise. for plot 2, level 1 is higher than level 2 for all four bars. For plot 3, neither level consisitently dominates. This is additional evidence that factors 1 and 2 are important, and this importance is robust across the various settings of all the factors.

  3. Interactions: Although there is some evidence of 2-term interactions in all of the plots, the effects are all considerably smaller than the factor 1 and factor 2 main effects.
