<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%">
<i>Exact binomial confidence limits</i>
</td>

<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%">
If the <A HREF="prc241.htm">sample size, <i>N</i>, is small</A>,
confidence limits that are approximated using the normal
distribution may not be accurate enough for some applications.  An
"exact" method based on the binomial distribution is discussed on
this page.  
</td>
</tr>

<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%">
<i>Constrution of exact two-sided confidence intervals based on
the binomial distribution</i>
</td>

<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%">
To construct a two-sided confidence interval at the
100(1 - <img align=absmiddle src="gifs/alpha.gif">)% confidence
level for the true proportion defective <i>p</i> where
<i>N<sub>d</sub></i> defects are found in a sample of
size <i>N</i> follow the steps below.
<p>
<OL>
   <LI>Solve the equation
       <p> <center>
       <img SRC="gifs/pueq.gif" alt=
"SUM[k=0 to Nd][(N k)*p(U)**k*(1-p(U))**(N-k)] \= alpha/2">
       </center> <p>
       for <i>p<sub>U</sub></i> to obtain the upper
       100(1-<img align=absmiddle src="gifs/alpha.gif">)% limit for
       <i>p</i>.
       <p>
   <LI>Next solve the equation
       <center> <p>
       <img SRC="gifs/pleq.gif" alt=
"SUM[k=0 to Nd-1][(N k)*p(L)**k*(1-p(L))**(N-k)] \= 1 - alpha/2">
      </center> <p>
      for <i>p<sub>L</sub></i> to obtain the lower 
      100(1 - <img align=absmiddle src="gifs/alpha.gif" alt="alpha">)%
      limit for <i>p</i>.
</OL>

</td>
</tr>

<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%">
<i>Note</i>
</td>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%">
The interval {<i>p<sub>L</sub></i>, <i>p<sub>U</sub></i>} is an exact
100(1 - <img align=absmiddle src="gifs/alpha.gif" alt="alpha">)%
confidence interval for <i>p</i>.  However, it is not symmetric
about the observed proportion defective,
<img align=absmiddle src="gifs/pnd.gif" alt="phat = Nd/N">.
</td>
</tr>

<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%">
<i>Example of calculation of upper limit for binomial confidence
intervals using EXCEL</i>
</td>

<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%">
The equations above that determine <i>p<sub>L</sub></i> and 
<i>p<sub>U</sub></i> can easily be solved using functions built
into EXCEL.  Take as an example the situation where twenty units
are sampled from a continuous production line and four items are
found to be defective.  The proportion defective is estimated to be
<img align=middle SRC="gifs/phat.gif"alt="phat"> = 4/20 = 0.20.
The calculation of a 90% confidence interval for the true proportion
defective, <i>p</i>, is demonstrated using EXCEL spreadsheets.
</td>
</tr>

<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%">
<i>Upper confidence limit from EXCEL</i>
</td>

<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%">
To solve for <i>p<sub>U</sub></i>:
<ol>
   <li>Open an EXCEL spreadsheet and put the starting value of 0.5
       in the A1 cell.
       <p>
   <li>Put =BINOMDIST(<i>Nd</i>, <i>N</i>, <i>A1</i>, <i>TRUE</i>)
       in B1, where <i>Nd</i> = 4  and <i>N</i> = 20.
       <p>
   <li>Open the Tools menu and click on GOAL SEEK.  The GOAL SEEK
       box requires 3 entries.
       <p>
       <UL>
          <li>B1 in the "Set Cell" box
          <li><img align=absmiddle
              src="gifs/alpha.gif" alt="alpha">/2
              = 0.05 in the "To Value" box
          <li>A1 in the "By Changing Cell" box.
       </UL>
       <p>
       The picture below shows the steps in the procedure.
</ol>
</td>
</tr>

<i>Example of calculation of lower limit for binomial confidence
limits using EXCEL</i>

<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%">

<i>p<sub>L</sub></i>:
<ol>

       <p>
       <UL>

<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%">
<i>Interpretation of result</i>
</td>

<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%">
A 90% confidence interval for the proportion defective, p, is
{0.071, 0.400}.  Whether or not the interval is truly "exact"
depends on the software.  Notice in the screens above that GOAL SEEK
is not able to find upper and lower limits that correspond to exact
0.05 and 0.95 confidence levels; the calculations are correct to two
significant digits which is probably sufficient for confidence
intervals.  The calculations using a package called SEMSTAT agree
with the EXCEL results to two significant digits.
</td>
</tr>

<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%">
<i>Calculations using SEMSTAT</i>

This computation can also be performed using the following
Dataplot program.
<PRE>
. Initalize
let p = 0.5
let nd = 4
let n = 20
. Define the functions
let function fu = bincdf(4,p,20) - 0.05
let function fl = bincdf(3,p,20) - 0.95
. Calculate the roots
let pu = roots fu wrt p  for p = .01 .99
let pl = roots fl wrt p  for p = .01 .99
. print the results
let pu1 = pu(1)
let pl1 = pl(1)
print "PU = ^pu1"
print "PL = ^pl1"
</PRE>
