<TD VALIGN=top>
<H2>Confidence intervals for large sample sizes</H2>
</TD>
</TR >
</TABLE>
</TD>
</TR>








<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%"><!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<i>Confidence intervals are another way of testing hypotheses</i>
</td>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%"><!-- Add main text below -->
Confidence intervals that bracket the population proportion
defective, <i>p</i>, are an alternative method for testing
hypotheses regarding proportion defectives.  Confidence intervals
corresponding to the <a href="prc24.htm">hypotheses tests on the
previous page</A> at a
100(1-<img align=absmiddle src="gifs/alpha.gif" alt="alpha">)%
confidence level are expressed as
<p>
<ol>
<!-- <img SRC="gifs/ci6.gif"> -->
   <li>Two-sided confidence interval for p
       <p>
       <img align=absmiddle src="eqns/p2s.gif" alt=
"phat - SQRT(phat*(1-phat)/N)*z(alpha/2) <= p <=
 phat + SQRT(phat*(1-phat)/N)*z(alpha/2)">
       <p>
   <li>Lower one-sided confidence interval for p
       <p>
       <img align=absmiddle src="eqns/p1l.gif"
       alt="p >= phat - SQRT(phat*(1-phat)/N)*z(alpha)">
       <p>
   <li>Upper one-sided confidence interval for p
       <p>
       <img align=absmiddle src="eqns/p1u.gif"
       alt="p <= phat + SQRT(phat*(1-phat)/N)*z(alpha)">
</ol>
<p>
where <img align=absmiddle SRC="gifs/phat.gif" alt="phat"> is the
estimate of the proportion defective from a sample of <i>N</i>; and
<img align=absmiddle SRC="gifs/zalpha.gif" alt="z(alpha)"> is
the <a href="../../eda/section3/eda3671.htm">upper critical value
from the normal distribution</A> that is exceeded with probability
<img align=absmiddle SRC="gifs/alpha.gif" alt="alpha">.  
</TD>
</TR>

<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%">
<i>Important restriction</i>
</td>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%">
These confidence intervals are approximate and are based on a
normal approximation to the binomial distribution which is valid
if <i>N</i> is large enough.  We can use the same rule-of-thumb
for determining whether or not <i>N</i> is sufficiently large
as was used in <a href="prc24.htm#ruleofthumb">preceding
section</a>.
<p>
Methods for constructing <a href="prc242.htm">confidence intervals
where <i>N</i> is small</a> are shown on the next page.
<a href="prc243.htm">Criteria for choosing a sample size</a> in
order to guarantee detecting a change of size
<img align=absmiddle src="gifs/delta.gif" alt="delta"> are
discussed on another page.
</td>
</tr>

<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%">
<i>Example of one-sided confidence interval</i>
</td>

<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%">
The 95% one-sided confidence interval for <i>p</i><sub>0</sub>
for the example from the <a href="prc24.htm#example">previous
page</a> is:
<p>
<center>
<img SRC="gifs/ci6ex.gif" alt=
"p >= phat - SQRT(phat*(1-phat)/N)*z(alpha;
   => p >= 0.13 - SQRT(0.13*0.87/200)*1.654;
   => p >= 0.0907">
</center>
<p>
Because the hypothesized value of <i>p</i><sub>0</sub> = 0.10 is
consistent with the confidence interval, the null hypothesis of
no change in the level of proportion defective is accepted.
</td>
</tr>

<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%">
<i>Correction factor for sampling from a finite population without
replacement</i>
</td>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%">
Thus far, we have assumed that the sample comes from a continuous
production line or a very large or infinite population.   If,
however, sampling is from a finite population and is done without
replacement, a correction factor is applied to the confidence
limits.  The correction factor is
<p>
<center>
<img src="gifs/fcf.gif" alt="SQRT((N' - N)/(N' - 1))">
</center>
<p>
where <i>N</i>' is the number of units in the population.  The 
corrected 100(1-<img align=absmiddle src="gifs/alpha.gif"
alt="alpha">)% confidence intervals become
<p>
<ol>
   <li>Two-sided confidence interval for p
       <p>
       <img align=absmiddle src="eqns/p2sc.gif" alt=
"phat - SQRT((N'-N)/(N'-1))*SQRT(phat*(1-phat)/N)*z(alpha/2) <= p <=
 phat + SQRT((N'-N)/(N'-1))*SQRT(phat*(1-phat)/N)*z(alpha/2)">
       <p>
   <li>Lower one-sided confidence interval for p
       <p>
       <img align=absmiddle src="eqns/p1lc.gif" alt=
"p >= phat - SQRT((N'-N)/(N'-1))*SQRT(phat*(1-phat)/N)*z(alpha)">
       <p>
   <li>Upper one-sided confidence interval for p
       <p>
       <img align=absmiddle src="eqns/p1uc.gif" alt=
"p <= phat + ((N'-N)/(N'-1))*SQRT(phat*(1-phat)/N)*z(alpha)">
</ol>
<!-- <img SRC="gifs/ci7.gif"> -->
</center>
<p>
If the population is extremely large, the correction factor will
be close to one.  As the sample size approaches the population size,
the correction factor approaches zero.

</td>
</tr>
