  1. The R output displays the results of a two-sided, one-sample t-test including: the t-test statistic value, t, the degrees of freedom, df, and the p-value associated with the test statistic. Also shown are a 95 % confidence interval for the mean and the sample mean.

  3. The test statistic for our example is 2611.286, which falls within the rejection region for two alternative hypotheses, and . Thus, we would reject the null hypothesis and accept the alternative, concluding that the population mean is not equal to 5 for the two-tailed test and that the population mean is greater than 5 for the one-tailed test.

  4. Another way to test the hypothesis is to compare the p-value associated with the test statistic to the level of the test, alpha. In our example, the p-value is listed as being less than 2.2e-16. Since the p-value is much less than our significance level of 0.05, we would reject the null hypothesis and conclude that the two means are not equal. Comparing the p-value to the significance level eliminates the need to look up t-table values for the critical region.

  5. The confidence interval listed in the R output provides an alternative to the hypothesis test. If the confidence interval contains 5, then the null hypothesis cannot be rejected. In our example, the confidence interval (9.25824, 9.26468) does not contain 5, indicating that the population mean does not equal 5 at the 0.05 level of significance.
