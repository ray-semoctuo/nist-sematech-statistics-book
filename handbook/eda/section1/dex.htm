EDA on DEX: Exploratory Data Analysis Techniques as Applied to 2-level Fractional Factorial Designs The virtues of EDA (Exploratory Data Analysis) were discussed in detail in Chapter 1. The following section presents the application of such EDA techniques to an extremely important sub-class of experiment designs: 2-level full and fractional factorial designs. This popular class of designs finds heavy usage for screening experiments: experiments to determine important factors and best settings in a multi-factor system. The standard classical analysis of the data from such designs makes heavy use of ANOVA (Analysis of Variance), whose application and validity requires 3 assumptions: 1. A behind-the-scenes model; 2. Normality; 3. Homoscedasticity (constant variation). Violations of these assumptions may potentially We present here an alternative EDA approach which has the following advantages: 1. No model is assumed (as required by ANOVA). 2. The analysis does not use a model; rather, EDA outputs a (data-based) model. 3. The procedure is graphical (and hence) more easily understood. 4. Assumptions of normality and homoscedasticity are not needed. 4. The assumption of normality is not required. 5. The assumption of homoscedasticity is not required. 5. The determination of important factors are direct and intuitive. 6. The determination of best settings are direct and intuitive. 7. Outliers are easily detected. 8. The battery of multiple EDA procedures leading to identical conclusions (analysis redundancy) remove approach-dependency and add conclusions-support.
