<H2>Hotelling's <i>T</i> squared</H2>
</TD>
</TR >
</TABLE>
</TD>
</TR>





<!-- begin paragraph -->
<TR>
<TD WIDTH=15% VALIGN=top>
<!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<I>
</I>
</TD>
<TD VALIGN=TOP WIDTH=85%>
<!-- Add main text below -->
<a NAME="The Hotelling   T2"></a>
<b>The Hotelling <i>T</i> <sup>2</sup> distance</b>

</TD>
</TR>
<!-- end paragraph -->

<!-- begin paragraph -->
<TR>
<TD WIDTH=15% VALIGN=top>
<!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<i>Measures "distance" from target using covariance matrix</i>
</TD>
<TD VALIGN=TOP WIDTH=85%>
<!-- Add main text below -->
A measure that takes into account multivariate covariance structure
was proposed by Harold Hotelling in 1947 and is called Hotelling's
<i>T</i> <sup>2</sup>.  It may be thought of as the multivariate
counterpart of the Student's <i>t</i>.  The <i>T</i><sup> 2</sup>
distance is a constant multiplied times a <i>quadratic form</i>.
For <u>one</u> group (or subgroup), this quadratic form
is obtained as follows:

</TD>
</TR>
<!-- end paragraph -->

<!-- begin paragraph -->
<TR>
<TD WIDTH=15% VALIGN=top>
<!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<i>Definition of T<sup> 2</sup></i>
</TD>
<TD VALIGN=TOP WIDTH=85%>
<!-- Add main text below -->
Start from the data matrix, <b>X</b><sub><i>n</i>x<i>p</i></sub>
(<i>n </i>x<i> p</i> element observations)
<ul>
   <li>Define a 1 x <i>p</i> target vector, <b>m</b>
       <p>
   <li>Compute the 1 x <i>p</i> vector of column averages,
       <b><i>x</i></b>
       <p>
   <li>Compute the covariance matrix, <b>S</b> and its inverse,
       <b>S</b><sup>-1</sup>.  Here <b>S</b> is the unbiased
       covariance estimate defined by
       <p>
       <ul>
       <!-- <img SRC="eqns/scovar.gif" height=45 width=194 ALT= -->
       <img SRC="eqns/hotel1b.gif" height=45 width=205 ALT=
"S(jh) = (1/(n-1))*SUM[i=1 to n](X(ij)-Xbar(j))*(X(ih)-Xbar(h))">
       </ul>
       <p>
   <li>Compute the matrix of deviations <b>(X-m )</b> and
       its transpose, <b>(X-m)<sup>'</sup></b>
       <p>
   <li>The quadratic form is computed by
       <b>Q</b> = <b>(X-m)<sup>'</sup>S</b><sup>-1</sup><b>(X-m)</b>
</ul>
The formula for the Hotelling <i>T</i><sup> 2</sup> is:
<ul>
   <i>T</i><sup> 2</sup> = <i>n</i><b>Q</b> = <i>n</i>
   <b>(x-m)'S</b><sup>-1</sup><b>(x-m)</b>
</ul>
The constant <i>n</i> is the size of the sample from which the
covariance matrix was estimated.  If no targets are available, use
the grand mean, which for one subgroup is the mean of the <i>p</i>
column means.

</TD>
</TR>
<!-- end paragraph -->

<!-- begin paragraph -->
<TR>
<TD WIDTH=15% VALIGN=top>
<!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<I>
Theoretical covariance matrix is diagonal
</I>
</TD>
<TD VALIGN=TOP WIDTH=85%>
<!-- Add main text below -->
It should be mentioned that for independent variables the theoretical
covariance matrix is a diagonal matrix and Hotelling's
<i>T</i><sup> 2</sup> becomes proportional to the sum of the squared
standardized variables.
<p>
In general, the higher the <i>T</i><sup> 2</sup> value, the more
distant is the observation from the target.

</TD>
</TR>
<!-- end paragraph -->

<!-- begin paragraph -->
<TR>
<TD WIDTH=15% VALIGN=top>
<!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<I>
Estimation formulas for m subgroups, each having n observations
on p variables
</I>
</TD>
<TD VALIGN=TOP WIDTH=85%>
<!-- Add main text below -->
A summary of the estimation procedure for the mean vector and
the covariance matrix for more than one subgroup follows.
<p>
Let <i>m</i> be the number of available subgroups (or samples).
Each subgroup has <i>n</i> observations on <i>p</i> variables.  The
subgroup means and variances are calculated from
<p>
<center>
<img SRC="eqns/hotel1.gif" BORDER=0 height=124 width=266 ALT=
"xbar(jk) = (1/n)*SUM[i=1 to n][x(ijk)]  for j = 1, 2, ..., p and
 k = 1, 2, ..., m;
 s(jk)^2 = (1/(n-1)*SUM[i=1 to n][(x(ijk) - xbar(jk))^2]
 for j = 1, 2, ..., p and k = 1, 2, ..., m">
</center>
<p>
x<sub><i>ijk</i></sub> is the <i>i</i>th observation on the
<i>j</i>th variable in subgroup <i>k</i>.  The covariance between
variable <i>j</i> and variable <i>h</i> in subgroup <i>k</i> is:
<p>
<img SRC="eqns/hotel1a.gif" height=48 width=324 alt=
"s(jhk) = (1/(n-1))*SUM[i=1 to n]
[(x(ijk) - xbar(jk))*(x(ijk) - xbar(hk))]
 for k = 1, 2, ..., m and j not equal h">
<p>
The statistics
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/xbarjk.gif" alt="xbar(jl)">,
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/s2jk.gif" alt="s(jk)^2">, and
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/s2jhk.gif" alt="s(jhk)">
are averaged over all <i>m</i> samples:
<ul>
<!-- <img SRC="eqns/hotel2.gif" height=216 width=314> -->
<img SRC="eqns/xbarbarj.gif" ALT=
"xbarbar(j) = (1/m)*SUM[i=1 to m][xbar(jk)]   j = 1, 2, ..., p">
<p>
<img SRC="eqns/sbarj2.gif" ALT=
"sbar(j)^2 = (1/m)*SUM[i=1 to m][S(jk)^2]   j = 1, 2, ..., p">
</ul>
and
<ul>
<img SRC="eqns/sbarjk.gif" ALT=
"sbar(jk) = (1/m)*SUM[k=1 to m][S(jhk)]   j not equal h">
</ul>
The
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/xbarbarj2.gif" alt="(xbarbar(j)">
are the elements of the vector
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/xbarbar.gif" alt="(xbarbar">
and the <i>p</i> x <i>p</i> average of the sample covariance
matrix <b><i>S</i></b> is
<ul>
<!-- <img SRC="eqns/hotel3.gif" height=204 width=325> -->
<img SRC="eqns/smat.gif" ALT=
"{sbar(1)^2  sbar(12) sbar(13) .... sbar(13);
  sbar(12) sbar(2)^2 sbar(22) ... sbar(2p);
  sbar(13) sbar(23) sbar(3)^2 ... sbar(3p);
     ..... ;
  sbar(1p)  ...   sbar(p)^2]">
</ul>
<b><i>S</i></b> is an unbiased estimate of
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/sigmauc.gif" alt="SIGMA">
when the process is in control.

</TD>
</TR>
<!-- end paragraph -->

<!-- begin paragraph -->
<TR>
<TD WIDTH=15% VALIGN=top>
<!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<I>
T<sup> 2</sup> lends itself to graphical display
</I>
</TD>
<TD VALIGN=TOP WIDTH=85%>
<!-- Add main text below -->
The <i>T</i><sup> 2</sup> distances lend themselves readily to
graphical displays and, as a result, the <i>T</i><sup> 2</sup>-chart
is the most popular among all multivariate control charts.  Let us
examine how this works: For <b>one</b> group (or subgroup) we have:
<ul>
   <i>T</i><sup>2</sup> =
   <i>n</i>(<i><b>X</b></i> -
   <i><b>m</b></i>)' <b><i>S</i><sup>-1</sup></b>
   (<i><b>X</b></i> - <i><b>m</b></i>)
</ul>
where
<ul>
   <li><b><i>X</i></b> = (<i>x</i><sub>1</sub>, ...,
       <i>x<sub>p</sub></i> is an individual vector measurement
       <p>
   <li><b>m</b> = (m<sub>1</sub>, ... , m<sub>p</sub>)
       is the vector of targets
       <p>
   <li><b><i>S</i><sup>-1</sup></b> is the inverse of the
       covariance matrix
       <p>
   <li><i>n</i> is the sample size
</ul>
When observations are grouped into <i>k</i> (rational) subgroups,
each of size <i>n</i>, we can compute a statistic,
<i>T</i><sup>2</sup>, for each subgroup that measures the deviations
of the subgroup averages from the target vector <b>m</b> where
<ul>
   <li><img align=absmiddle src="eqns/t2m.gif" alt=
            "T(j)^2 = n*(xbar(j)-m)'*S(p)^(-1)*(xbar(j)-m)">
       <p>
   <li><img align=absmiddle src="eqns/xbarj.gif" alt="xbar(j)">
       is the 1x<i>p</i> vector of the averages of subgroup
       <i>j</i>
       <p>
   <li><img align=absmiddle src="eqns/sp.gif" alt=
            "S(p) = (1/k)*SUM[i=1 to k][S(j)]">
       is the pooled covariance matrix 
       <p>
   <li><b><i>m</i></b> is the 1x<i>p</i> target vector
       <p>
   <li><i>n</i> is the subgroup sample size
</ul>
<a NAME="eqns/hotel"></a>
<!-- <img SRC="eqns/hotel5.gif" height=153 width=298> -->
<!--  <img SRC="eqns/mvar9.gif" height=136 width=353> -->

</TD>
</TR>
<!-- end paragraph -->

<!-- begin paragraph -->
<TR>
<TD WIDTH=15% VALIGN=top>
<!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<i>Hotelling<br> T<sup> 2</sup> upper control limit (there is no
lower limit)</i>
</TD>
<TD VALIGN=TOP WIDTH=85%>
<!-- Add main text below -->
The Upper Control Limit (UCL) on the control chart is
<ul>
   <!-- <img align=absmiddle src="eqns/chi.gif" alt="Chi">
   <sup>2</sup>
   <sub> <img align=bottom src="eqns/alpha.gif" alt="alpha">,
   <i>p</i></sub> -->
   <img src="eqns/uclhot.gif" alt=
        "UCL = [p*(n-1)/(n-p)}*F(p,n-p;alpha)">
</ul>
where
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/fnnmp.gif" alt="F(p,n-p;alpha)">
is the upper 100
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/alpha.gif" alt="alpha">
percentile of the <i>F</i>-distribution with
<i>p</i> and <i>n</i>-<i>p</i> degrees of freedom.

</TD>
</TR>
<!-- end paragraph -->

<!-- begin paragraph -->
<TR>
<TD WIDTH=15% VALIGN=top>
<!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<I>
Case where no target is available
</I>
</TD>
<TD VALIGN=TOP WIDTH=85%>
<!-- Add main text below -->
If no target <b>m</b> is available, we may substitute the grand mean
vector of the averages of the sample mean vectors.  We also might
choose to do this when the grand mean vector differs more than
slightly from the target vector.  A target vector may be unrealistic
relative to the current state of a multivariate process.  In this
case the formula becomes
<ul>
   <!-- <img SRC="eqns/mvar12.gif" height=94 width=373 -->
   <img SRC="eqns/t2j2.gif"
     ALT="T(j)**2 = n*(Xbar(j) - Xbar)'*S(p)^(-1)*(Xbar(j) -Xbar)">
</ul>
where
<ul>
   <img align=absmiddle src="eqns/xbarbar2.gif"
        alt="Xbarbar = (1/k)*SUM[j=1 to k][xbar(j)]">
</ul>

</TD>
</TR>
<!-- end paragraph -->

<!-- begin paragraph -->
<TR>
<TD WIDTH=15% VALIGN=top>
<!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<I>
Upper control limit for no target case
</I>
</TD>
<TD VALIGN=TOP WIDTH=85%>
<!-- Add main text below -->
The Upper Control Limit (UCL) is given by
<ul>
<img SRC="eqns/mvar11.gif" height=94 width=214 ALT=
     "UCL = {p*(k-1)*(n-1)/(k*(n-1)+1-p)}*F(alpha,p,k*(n-1)+1-p)">
</ul>
where <img align=absmiddle SRC="eqns/uclhot3.gif"
ALT="F(alpha,p,k*(n-1)+1-p)"> is the upper 100
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/alpha.gif" alt="alpha">
percentile of the <i>F</i>-distribution with <i>p</i> and
<i>k</i>(<i>n</i>-<i>1</i>)+1-<i>p</i> degrees of freedom,
 <i>p</i> is the number of variables, <i>n</i> is the size
of the subgroup, and <i>k</i> is the number of subgroups in the
sample.  There is no lower control limit, as is the case for most
multivariate control charts.

</TD>
</TR>
<!-- end paragraph -->

<tr>
<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="15%"><!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<i>There is also a dispersion chart (similar to an s or R univariate
control chart)</i>
</td>

<td VALIGN=TOP WIDTH="85%"><!-- Add main text below -->
As in the univariate case, for grouped data, the
Hotelling T<sup> 2</sup> chart may be accompanied by a chart that
displays and monitors a measure of dispersion within the subgroups.
The statistic that fulfills this obligation is
<ul>
   <!-- <img SRC="eqns/mvar12a.gif" height=149 width=378 ALT= -->
   <img SRC="eqns/tdsq.gif" ALT=
"T(D)**2 = SUM[i=1 to n)(X(ij)-Xbar(j))'*S(p)**(-1)*(X(ij)-Xbar(j))">
</ul>
for each subgroup <i>j</i>.
<p>
The UCL for dispersion statistics is given by Alt (1985) as
<ul>
   <img SRC="eqns/dispucl.gif"
        ALT="Chi-Square(alpha,p*(p+1)/2)">
</ul>
</td>
</tr>

<!-- begin paragraph -->
<TR>
<TD WIDTH=15% VALIGN=top>
<!-- Add marginal notes below -->
<I>
Correspondence between univariate t and multivariate T<sup> 2</sup>
</I>
</TD>
<TD VALIGN=TOP WIDTH=85%>
<!-- Add main text below -->
To illustrate the correspondence between the univariate <i>t</i> and
the multivariate T<sup>2</sup> observe the following:
<ul>
<img SRC="eqns/hotel12.gif" height=44 width=66
     alt="t = (xbar - u0)/s/SQRT(N)">
</ul>
follows a <i>t</i> distribution.  If we test the hypothesis that
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/mu.gif" alt="mu"> =
<img align=absmiddle src="eqns/mu.gif" alt="mu"><sub>0</sub> we get:
<ul>
<img SRC="eqns/hotel12.gif" height=44 width=66
     alt="t = (xbar - mu0)/(s/SQRT(N))">
</ul>
then
<ul>
<img SRC="eqns/t2a.gif"
     alt="t^2 = (xbar - mu0)^2/(s^2/n)">
</ul>
or
<ul>
<img SRC="eqns/t2b.gif"
     alt="t^2 = n(xbar - mu0*(s^2)^(-1)(xbar - mu0)">
</ul>
<!-- <img SRC="eqns/hotel14.gif" height=172 width=180> -->
<p>
Generalizing to <i>p</i> variables yields
<a href="#hotel">Hotelling's T<sup>2</sup></a>.

</TD>
</TR>
